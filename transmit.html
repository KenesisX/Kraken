🖼 Image transmission & streaming (phone / Pi / desktop)

image-mode reference
SSTV — analog still image, pixel brightness→audio tone over a ~3 kHz SSB channel (Martin, Robot36, Scottie, PD).
Digital SSTV (HamDRM) — sends the whole file (JPEG2000/PNG), higher res, no pixel smear (QSSTV/EasyPal).
WEFAX — analog weather-fax; digitize to a screen (fldigi).
ATV/DATV — fast-scan real-time video (NTSC/PAL or digital YUV/RGB) to a TV or matrix.
KG-STV / PASOKON / MSCAN — digital image/video modes rendered on a PC/phone.
Wi-Fi / LoRa / BLE — stream JPEG/RTSP/WebM to your own screen; LoRa for long-range compressed stills, BLE for short range.
Pick a mode whose output your display can render (raw video, JPEG file, or digital stream).

▲ Transmit

Transmitting is legal only for a licensed operator, on the amateur bands above, within your licence class & power limits, with station ID. You are responsible for compliance (FCC Part 97 / your national equivalent). Kraken caps power and refuses out-of-band frequencies, but the licence and the airtime are yours.
Not included, by design: replay / copy / clone / "replicate" of captured signals, and any transmit on cellular (2G–5G/GSM), GNSS, or satphone spectrum. Retransmitting captured RF and transmitting on licensed cellular/satellite bands are illegal regardless of context. This page is clean HAM TX only — Signal (page 3) still does full RX/detect/measure on every band.

⇅ Concurrent channels — full-duplex / multi-radio (RX+TX, TX+TX, different freqs & modes)

Simultaneous operation needs one physical radio per active channel, or a full-duplex SDR (USRP / BladeRF / LimeSDR). A single HackRF is half-duplex — it can't TX and RX at the same instant, so give each concurrent channel its own device (⚠ flags a shared radio). TX channels still require the gold arm + a valid callsign + an amateur band; RX channels run freely on any frequency.

📻 Baofeng / CHIRP — program · receive · copy · transmit

CHIRP (free, open-source) reads a radio's codeplug over a USB programming cable and writes it to another — this copies channel programming (frequency, CTCSS T-CTCS / DCS T-DCS tone, power, offset), not over-the-air signals. TX keys the radio only when armed on an amateur/GMRS channel you're licensed for.
